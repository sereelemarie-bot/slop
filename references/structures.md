# Formulaic Structures

Phrases are easy to grep for. Structures are harder — they're patterns of
thought, not words, and they're what actually makes prose sound generated.
Watch for these five.

## Binary contrasts

"It's not X, it's Y." "This isn't just about X — it's about Y." The contrast
does no work; it's a rhetorical costume for a plain statement. State Y and
drop the setup entirely.

**Before:** "This isn't just a bug fix — it's a rethink of how the queue
handles retries."
**After:** "The fix rethinks how the queue handles retries."

## Negative listings

"Not slower, not less secure, not harder to maintain — just better." Piling
up things something *isn't* stalls the sentence instead of advancing it.
Say what it is.

**Before:** "The new API isn't more complex, isn't slower, and doesn't
require more setup."
**After:** "The new API has one endpoint and no setup step."

## Dramatic fragmentation

Short. Punchy. Fragments. For effect. One of these lands; three in a row
reads like a trailer voiceover. Use a fragment when a fragment is the
clearest way to say the thing, not as a rhythm section.

**Before:** "The server crashed. No warning. No logs. Nothing."
**After:** "The server crashed without warning, and left no logs."

## Rhetorical setups

Questions the writer immediately answers themselves. "So what does this
mean for you?" "Why does this matter?" These exist to fake momentum. Cut
the question and just say the answer.

**Before:** "So why does this matter? Because slow builds cost engineers
hours every week."
**After:** "Slow builds cost engineers hours every week."

## False agency

Giving an abstraction a verb that belongs to a person. "The data speaks for
itself." "The design decision emerges from constraints." "The complaint
becomes a fix." Nothing emerges, speaks, or becomes on its own — someone did
it. Name them.

**Before:** "The bug report eventually becomes a patch."
**After:** "An engineer read the bug report and wrote a patch."

## How to check

Read each paragraph and ask: is this contrast/list/fragment/question
actually necessary, or is it here because it "sounds like writing"? If a
sentence's subject is an idea, a process, or a document instead of a person,
rewrite it around the person doing the thing.
