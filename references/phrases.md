# Filler Phrases

Cut these on sight. None of them carry information — they're padding that makes
prose sound like it's warming up instead of saying something.

## Throat-clearing openers

Delete the windup and start with the point.

- "It's important to note that..."
- "It's worth mentioning that..."
- "Let's dive into..."
- "Let's explore..."
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "When it comes to X..."
- "At the end of the day..."
- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what you need to know:"
- "The truth is..."
- "Simply put..."
- "Needless to say..."
- "As we all know..."
- "One thing to keep in mind is..."

**Before:** "It's important to note that the migration will take about an hour."
**After:** "The migration takes about an hour."

## Emphasis crutches

Words that try to manufacture weight instead of earning it. If the sentence
needs one of these to feel important, the sentence isn't important — fix the
sentence.

- "It's crucial/essential/vital that..."
- "This is a game-changer."
- "This cannot be overstated."
- "It goes without saying..."
- "Without a doubt..."
- "In no uncertain terms..."
- "This is a testament to..."
- "This underscores/highlights/showcases..."
- "This serves as a reminder that..."
- "It's a powerful reminder..."

**Before:** "This underscores the importance of testing before deploy."
**After:** "Test before you deploy."

## Adverbs

Cut all of them. An adverb is almost always compensating for a weak verb or a
vague claim. Find the stronger verb or the concrete detail instead.

- "significantly," "substantially," "considerably"
- "fundamentally," "essentially," "basically"
- "incredibly," "remarkably," "notably"
- "clearly," "obviously," "simply"
- "actually," "really," "very"
- "ultimately," "effectively," "arguably"
- "importantly," "interestingly," "surprisingly"

**Before:** "The change significantly improves performance and is incredibly
easy to adopt."
**After:** "The change cuts load time in half. Teams adopted it in a day."

## How to check

Search the draft for: "note that," "important," "crucial," "game-changer,"
"testament," "underscores," and any word ending in "-ly." Cut or replace every
hit. If a sentence goes limp without the adverb, the sentence was never doing
the work — rewrite it with a concrete verb or fact instead.
